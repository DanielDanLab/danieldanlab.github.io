{Stop-word removal}{stop-words-removal}

You should already be aware that the most frequent word in English
texts, is the article {the}. Comparing the word frequencies of two different texts with
different topics, we would notice that the first
50 words would be likely to have similar frequencies. Such words that appear
very frequently but are also uninformative, we repeat this again,
are called stop words (Fox 1989). Once the {tm} library is loaded,
a complete list of the stop words can be found by typing
{stopwords()} in R's console. As you can notice, the list
includes contracted form such as {isn't}. This tells us that we
need to remove the stop words {before} eliminating the punctuation. To
remove the stop words (and others if needed) from our text, we use the
{removeWords()} function.

{Program\ code\ starts}

{Shaded}
{Highlighting}[]
{\# remove the stopwords and specify the language}
{docs }{{}{-}} {tm\_map}{(docs, }{content\_transformer}{(removeWords), }{stopwords}{(}{"english"}{))}

{Removing other words}{own-dictionary-words-removal}

Sometimes we might need to remove other additional words. This process
is often accompanied by some trial and error, along with
informed decision-making. In a dataset where a city name appears
extensively, it becomes so prevalent in the corpus that we would like to
remove it because it will not contribute any additional insights. The
same {removeWords()} function can be used again. Suppose we
decide to remove the word {will} from our text.


{Shaded}
{Highlighting}[]
{\# Remove specific words from the corpus}
{docs }{{}{-}} {tm\_map}{(docs, }{content\_transformer}{(removeWords), }{c}{(}{"will"}{))}
